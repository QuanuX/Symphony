# SCV Finite Composition Contract v1

## Scope and owner

The additive `0.6.0-dev` native operations `composition_explore` and `composition_reassess` evaluate finite, caller-authored recipe combinations and retain exact evidence and explanations. The existing source, corpus, interpretation and connection protocols remain unchanged. This companion owns `composition.schema.json`. It describes a bounded SCV tool, not rules for every Symphony user's program or a complete Composer.

SCV enumerates alternatives itself. qxctl supplies exact installation selection, discovery, bounded invocation and optional evidence retention. Neither operation acquires sources, runs a probe, chooses a provider, changes a requirement, grants authority or deploys anything. The same interfaces serve human and permitted agent callers.

## Exact evidence and requirements

Explore input is exactly `{interpretations,additional_knowledge,provider_packs,query_time,requirements,slots,allowed_guarantee_changes,counterfactuals,bounds}`. Ordinary interpretations and knowledge retain their existing contracts. Each selected provider pack evaluation is retained in full and exactly replayed through its owner before its ordinary knowledge enters evaluation. Duplicate pack evaluations fail. The composed collection remains subject to the existing common-policy, capture, claim and total-artifact limits. Coverage reports are inventory evidence; they are not substituted for these semantic inputs.

One native connection evaluation builds the shared graph assessment at the caller's exact UTC query time. Candidate and counterfactual checks use that assessment through an internal helper; there is no new operation accepting an untrusted external assessment. Exact native replay and independent qxctl checks remain distinct validation layers.

Each common requirement is `{requirement_id,importance,operator,right,resolution}`. Importance is explicitly `required` or `optional`; operators and right operands use the existing connection-check contract, including exact types, units and scoped claim references. All candidates and counterfactuals retain the same requirement identities. Recipe-to-requirement mappings are explicitly authored: matching a referenced claim does not prove that the recipe actually realizes that capability. The caller supplies a resolution descriptor `{kind,reference,description}` with kind `source`, `observation`, `adapter` or `caller_decision`. References are opaque identifiers or locations for subsequent review. They are never executed, fetched or treated as permission.

## Recipes and finite search

A slot is `{slot_id,allowed_provider_ids,recipes}`. Its allowed provider IDs are an explicit nonempty caller selection. Recipe provider labels describe the caller's model; they allocate no new native engine or provider authority. A recipe is exactly `{recipe_id,provider_id,bindings,prerequisites,requires_interfaces,supplies_interfaces,guarantee_changes,implementation,resolution}`.

A binding `{requirement_id,claim}` maps a common requirement to one existing exact claim reference. Unknown requirement IDs or duplicate bindings inside one recipe fail. Zero bindings for a requirement in a candidate remain unresolved; multiple selected recipes binding the same requirement remain ambiguous and unresolved. A satisfied prerequisite cannot silently remove an unbound mandatory requirement. Prerequisites are ordinary connection checks with their own explicit importance.

Interface entries are exact caller-supplied contract references. The search checks whether the selected recipes collectively declare each required reference among their supplied references. Equality establishes a declared match only. It does not establish semantic completeness, live connectivity, initialization order, payload delivery, throughput, shared quotas or a functioning distributed composition. No assumption of acyclic topology or universally correct architecture is imposed. Missing declared interfaces create explicit obligations.

Guarantee-change IDs describe authored transformations. A recipe is excluded when its provider is outside its slot's selected provider IDs or it requests a guarantee change absent from `allowed_guarantee_changes`. Every excluded recipe and reason stays visible. The remaining choices are sorted by slot and recipe identity and enumerated as the Cartesian product of one recipe per slot. The engine does not merely accept a list of completed plans. It does not rank candidates or optimize an unstated preference.

Implementation is `{status,reference}`, with status `available`, `unimplemented` or `unknown`. An available declaration requires a non-null reference. These fields remain the caller's assertion about an implementation; neither the reference nor recipe selection proves installed runtime behavior. Every selected recipe consequently retains an implementation-verification or implementation-gap obligation. Such declarations do not become documented provider facts.

Search bounds are explicit: one to four slots, one to eight recipes per slot, one to 32 common requirements, at most 16 prerequisites and 32 bindings per recipe, at most 16 required/supplied interfaces and guarantee changes per recipe, at most 32 allowed guarantee changes, and `bounds.max_candidates` from one to 32. Slot IDs are bounded to 128 UTF-8 bytes; other new identifiers are at most 512 bytes with no ASCII controls. Resolution descriptions are at most 2,048 bytes. Existing byte/depth/value, total native-check and evidence limits can reject a request that meets individual array counts.

The result reports `requested_combinations`, `eligible_combinations`, `evaluated_candidates`, `exhaustive`, `stop_reason` and `excluded_recipes`. Requested combinations count the unfiltered slot product; eligible combinations count the product after explicit caller filters. Stop reason is `exhausted`, `candidate_limit` or `no_eligible_recipe`. Exhaustiveness concerns that finite declared model only. The existing process deadline remains a hard error, not a fabricated partial success or a new wall-clock guarantee.

## Results and counterfactuals

The sealed result is `symphony.scv.composition-exploration.v1` and retains `{protocol,domain,input,evidence_evaluation,evidence_obligations,search,scenarios,limitations,digest}`. Each scenario has its ID, changed requirement IDs and candidates. Baseline uses reserved ID `baseline`. Candidate identity is a digest of the exact sorted `{slot_id,recipe_id,provider_id}` choices, allowing comparison across requirement changes without concealing recipe revisions in the retained input.

Each candidate retains choices, native connections, unbound requirements, interface matches, referenced claim IDs, explicit obligations, a check status and a separate implementation status. Required native checks, required unbound mappings and missing declared interfaces determine check status with precedence `contradicted`, `unresolved`, `conditional`, `satisfied`; no required checks yields unresolved. Optional findings remain visible. The aggregate implementation status is `unimplemented` if any chosen recipe is unimplemented, otherwise `unknown` if any is unknown, otherwise `available` as declared. A candidate may satisfy its documented checks while remaining unimplemented. Its obligations make that distinction inspectable.

Missing, expired, conflicting or ineligible evidence yields unresolved support rather than provider impossibility. A contradiction remains scoped to the exact selected evidence and requirement. Conditional evidence retains its comparison and qualification. Obligations bind the relevant requirement or recipe, claim references and caller resolution descriptor; native check findings supply the detailed reason and source evidence. They identify work to inspect or implement without automatically authorizing it.

At most four optional counterfactuals contain `{counterfactual_id,requirements}`. Each supplies a complete alternative requirement set with exactly the baseline identities. All use the same selected evidence, query time, recipes, provider filters, transformation permissions and enumerated candidate choices. A counterfactual cannot silently relax a provider or transformation selection. Changed requirement IDs are explicit. No minimum-edit, optimality or causal claim is made, and no relaxation is applied to the baseline.

Selected pack conformance failures and unrun fixtures remain in shared `evidence_obligations`, keyed by exact pack-evaluation digest and fixture ID with produced claim IDs. Candidates whose authored bindings, right-hand requirements or evaluated checks directly reference those claims additionally carry `evidence_obligation_refs`, including ambiguous or unbound requirements. These findings require review of the selected mapping and fixture; they do not automatically ban a provider or rewrite caller policy. Unreferenced fixture failures remain globally inspectable.

## Exact reassessment

Reassess input is `{before,after}`, containing complete exploration results. Both are fully replayed before comparison. The result `symphony.scv.composition-reassessment.v1` retains its exact input, both digests, change axes, union candidate records, search-change flag and limitations. Axes are `evidence`, `policy`, `requirements`, `query_time`, `recipes`, `selections` and `bounds`.

Evidence compares exact supplied interpretation, ordinary-knowledge and pack-evaluation arrays, so a selected artifact's profile or policy revision can also change that axis. Policy separately compares the composed evidence policy. Requirements include baseline and counterfactual selections; recipe and provider/guarantee selections are compared separately. Axes can overlap and are differences, not proof of causal attribution.

Candidate records are keyed by scenario and candidate identity. Changed compares the complete candidate result; affected additionally includes differences in the cycle-safe transitive closure of referenced graph findings, including primary and alternative support dependencies and their retained evidence qualification. The dependency inventory includes all selected recipe bindings and common right-hand claim operands, including ambiguous bindings and requirements whose left binding is missing. They remain unresolved while their evidence changes and pack-conformance obligations stay visible. Missing sides have null status. An absent candidate can result from a different search bound or caller filter; absence is not proof of impossibility. Prior artifacts remain unchanged.

## Acceptance

Owner tests cover actual Cartesian combinations, partial-search accounting, explicit provider and transformation permissions, repeated and missing requirements, ambiguous mappings, unavailable implementations, missing interfaces, pinned-evidence counterfactuals, expiry and exact-result tamper rejection. qxctl independently reconstructs enumeration, mapping and obligations and checks native typed comparisons, aggregation and reassessment attribution. Actual installed-process execution and package preservation are recorded separately; structural schemas and seals do not certify authored semantics or deployed behavior.
